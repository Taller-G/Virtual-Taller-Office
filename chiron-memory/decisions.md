# decision

A choice made and the reasoning behind it — the path taken over the alternatives.

## South wing added by growing the map canvas instead of filling the gaps

What: The three new rooms (South Meeting Room, East Meeting Room, Focus Room) were added by growing the Tiled canvas from 40×30 to 40×40 tiles and hanging them off the vertical corridor (cols 20-23), which was prolonged from row 28 to row 34 · Why: the free pockets inside the original 40×30 canvas were 3-5 tiles tall, too small for rooms with walls, doors and furniture; appending rows at the bottom keeps every existing object's coordinates untouched, so nothing in the old office moves · Where: apps/client/public/assets/map/first-office.json · Learned: extend a Tiled map to the right or the bottom — origin-anchored coordinates then stay valid and only the tile layers need re-padding

## Travelling joins the destination world before leaving the origin

What: Crossing a door does `join` (not `joinOrCreate`) on the destination room, waits for its first state, and only then leaves the origin room with a consented leave · Why: if the destination is down, full or its map failed, the traveller must stay exactly where they were with a message instead of ending up in limbo; `joinOrCreate` would silently resurrect a world the server deliberately did not start · Where: apps/client/src/network/connection.ts (`travelTo`) · Learned: for a hand-off between two live connections, acquire the new one first and release the old one last

## Doors are crossed by walking, detected client-side

What: Each frame the client intersects its own avatar's **physics body rectangle** (the feet: `body.top === avatar.y`, 18×12 px) with the map's `door` rectangles and starts the trip; there is no key press, prompt or server-side trigger · Why: the client already resolves collisions and owns the avatar's position, so it reacts instantly; the server still decides where the traveller lands (it resolves the named spawn in its own map) · Where: apps/client/src/game/OfficeScene.ts (`checkDoors`), packages/shared/src/map.ts (`doorAtRect`) · Learned: testing a single point against a one-tile door makes the trigger band a few pixels wide and doors feel dead — overlap the whole body; and the arriving player must be re-armed, so travel only triggers again after stepping out of a door area

## A dark world gets its own derived tileset, not a darker ambient veil

What: The Chiron Office is painted with `ChironDark.png`, generated by `tools/make-chiron-tileset.py` from the very tiles the First Office uses, run through a cold duotone (shadow `#070a14` → steel `#607290`, gamma 1.4); `ambient` stays low (`#4d080d1a`) and only tames the furniture, which still comes from the bright packs · Why: `FloorAndGround` (LimeZu Modern Interiors) is a bright pack — of its 1482 full tiles only 6 have luminance under 80, so there is nothing dark to build a moody world out of; raising the `ambient` veil instead greys everything out, avatars included, and the world still reads as the same place with the lights off · Where: tools/chiron_tiles.py, tools/make-chiron-tileset.py, apps/client/public/assets/tilesets/ChironDark.png · Learned: reuse the _source_ tiles of a world you know assembles correctly and recolour them — the wall corners, caps and shadow rows keep fitting, and only the palette changes

## The door between worlds is drawn as a dark doorway cut into the wall

What: Over each door rectangle there is a decorative two-tile `portal` sprite (from `ChironDark`) on the wall above it, plus a `threshold` glow on the floor tile; the First Office map embeds `ChironDark` just for those three tiles · Why: the client already draws a violet rectangle and the destination world's name over a door, but on its own that reads as a marker, not as a way out; a dark opening with cold light spilling from it is legible at a glance and says _which_ world is on the other side · Where: apps/client/public/assets/map/first-office.json (layer Furniture), tools/make-chiron-map.py (`portal`) · Learned: only the north wall of a room has a visible face in this art style — south and side walls are a one-tile line seen edge-on, so a doorway cut into them does not read; put the door in the north wall

## Sitting at a desk is a place in the map, not a feature of the app

What: "Being focused" is sitting at an object of class `seat` in the Tiled map (unique name, optional `dir`), and the whole state is one field, `Player.seatId`: focused _is_ holding a seat, and the status shown everywhere derives from it (`playerStatus()`) · Why: the alternative — a `focused` boolean plus a separate list of who occupies what — is two sources of truth that drift, and it would have put the office's furniture layout in code; with a seat as map data, adding a desk is adding an object in Tiled and seat occupancy comes free as room state, so whoever joins later sees who is sitting where · Where: packages/shared/src/map.ts (`CLASS_SEAT`, `findSeats`), packages/shared/src/schema.ts (`seatId`, `playerStatus`) · Learned: when a piece of state already answers two questions ("is this person focused?" and "is this seat taken?"), do not add a second field that can disagree with it

## Sitting down is the server's decision, unlike walking

What: `SIT` / `STAND` are requests; the server validates the seat, refuses one already taken, and writes `seatId`, position and facing itself — there is no rejection message, because a refusal is simply the state not changing and the client already draws from the state · Why: movement can stay client-authoritative (it is only ever about yourself), but a seat is contended: two people pressing the key at the same instant must not both get it · Where: apps/server/src/rooms/WorldRoom.ts (`onSit`, `onStand`) · Learned: the same app can have client-authoritative and server-authoritative state side by side — the dividing line is whether two clients can want the same thing

## A focus desk never marks you away by itself

What: the inactivity sweep skips anyone who is focused; only a manual "mark me away" can make a seated person away, and that frees the desk · Why: being heads-down without touching a key is exactly what a focus desk is for — evicting someone for using it as intended defeats the feature · Where: apps/server/src/rooms/WorldRoom.ts (`checkAway`, `onSetAway`) · Learned: an inactivity timer has to ask what the inactivity means before acting on it

## Tab opens the bubble chat only when the keyboard is on the game

What: The chat's document-level shortcut takes `Tab` as well as `Enter`, but `Tab` is only intercepted when `document.activeElement` is the body or the canvas (and no modifier is held); from any control — the name field, the away button — it goes on moving the focus as the browser intends · Why: `isTyping()` alone is not enough of a guard for `Tab`: it only covers text fields, so a button with the focus would have had its `Tab` stolen and the panel would have become unreachable by keyboard · Where: apps/client/src/ui/chat.ts (`onGame`, the `document` keydown listener) · Learned: when a plain key is turned into a shortcut, the question is not only "is someone typing?" but "is the key already doing a job here?" — `Tab` is the browser's way of walking the controls, so it can only be borrowed from the one place that has no controls

## The agent count is part of the synced identity, not a client-side setting

What: How many robot mascots walk behind someone is one number, `Player.agents`, sent as a join option, clamped once on the server (`sanitizeAgentCount`: not a finite number → 0, fractional truncated, outside `[0, MAX_AGENTS]` clamped) and replicated with `.default(0)`; every client draws that many robots for every player it can see, its own included · Why: the mascots have to be visible to everyone, and the only way for that to hold without a second channel is for the count to live where the name and the avatar already live — and when Chiron replaces the manual stepper, only the source of that one number changes, nothing downstream · Where: packages/shared/src/agents.ts, packages/shared/src/schema.ts (`agents`), apps/server/src/rooms/WorldRoom.ts (`onJoin`) · Learned: `.default(0)` on the new schema field is what lets a client that knows nothing about agents keep joining a room that does

## Mascots follow the owner's path, not the owner

What: Each player has a `MascotTrain` that records breadcrumbs of where its owner has been (one every 2 px) and puts robot _i_ at `(i + 1) * 14` px back **along that trail**, easing towards it at up to 185 px/s; they are plain sprites with no physics body, depth = their own feet · Why: steering each robot at the owner directly makes them cut corners and pile up in a clump on the inside of every turn, and giving them bodies would let a mascot wedge its owner in a doorway; a trail gives the queue, the corners and the even spacing for free, and the absence of a body is what guarantees they can never block anyone, break a bubble (the server computes those from players only) or trigger a door · Where: apps/client/src/game/MascotTrain.ts, apps/client/src/game/OfficeScene.ts (`mascots`) · Learned: the steering constant doubles as the lag — a robot chasing a target that flees at the walking speed settles `SPEED / CATCHUP` px behind it, so `CATCHUP` is chosen from how far back they should read while walking, not by feel

## Where the trail runs out, the robots huddle behind their owner

What: When the owner has not walked far enough for the trail to reach robot _i_ — they just spawned, or arrived through a door — that robot goes to a fallback place: behind the owner's facing, fanned out in rows of two · Why: without it every robot that falls off the end of the trail targets the same last point, so a player who has never moved stands on a single stack of robots; with it they spread out at once and slide into the line as soon as their owner walks · Where: apps/client/src/game/MascotTrain.ts (`huddle`) · Learned: a path-following follower needs an answer for "there is no path yet", and it is the only moment its geometry is not simply the path
