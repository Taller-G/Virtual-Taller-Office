# decision

A choice made and the reasoning behind it — the path taken over the alternatives.

## South wing added by growing the map canvas instead of filling the gaps

What: The three new rooms (South Meeting Room, East Meeting Room, Focus Room) were added by growing the Tiled canvas from 40×30 to 40×40 tiles and hanging them off the vertical corridor (cols 20-23), which was prolonged from row 28 to row 34 · Why: the free pockets inside the original 40×30 canvas were 3-5 tiles tall, too small for rooms with walls, doors and furniture; appending rows at the bottom keeps every existing object's coordinates untouched, so nothing in the old office moves · Where: apps/client/public/assets/map/first-office.json · Learned: extend a Tiled map to the right or the bottom — origin-anchored coordinates then stay valid and only the tile layers need re-padding

## Travelling joins the destination world before leaving the origin

What: Crossing a door does `join` (not `joinOrCreate`) on the destination room, waits for its first state, and only then leaves the origin room with a consented leave · Why: if the destination is down, full or its map failed, the traveller must stay exactly where they were with a message instead of ending up in limbo; `joinOrCreate` would silently resurrect a world the server deliberately did not start · Where: apps/client/src/network/connection.ts (`travelTo`) · Learned: for a hand-off between two live connections, acquire the new one first and release the old one last

## Doors are crossed by walking, detected client-side

What: Each frame the client intersects its own avatar's **physics body rectangle** (the feet: `body.top === avatar.y`, 18×12 px) with the map's `door` rectangles and starts the trip; there is no key press, prompt or server-side trigger · Why: the client already resolves collisions and owns the avatar's position, so it reacts instantly; the server still decides where the traveller lands (it resolves the named spawn in its own map) · Where: apps/client/src/game/OfficeScene.ts (`checkDoors`), packages/shared/src/map.ts (`doorAtRect`) · Learned: testing a single point against a one-tile door makes the trigger band a few pixels wide and doors feel dead — overlap the whole body; and the arriving player must be re-armed, so travel only triggers again after stepping out of a door area

## A dark world gets its own derived tileset, not a darker ambient veil

What: The Chiron Office is painted with `ChironDark.png`, generated by `tools/make-chiron-tileset.py` from the very tiles the First Office uses, run through a cold duotone (shadow `#070a14` → steel `#607290`, gamma 1.4); `ambient` stays low (`#4d080d1a`) and only tames the furniture, which still comes from the bright packs · Why: `FloorAndGround` (LimeZu Modern Interiors) is a bright pack — of its 1482 full tiles only 6 have luminance under 80, so there is nothing dark to build a moody world out of; raising the `ambient` veil instead greys everything out, avatars included, and the world still reads as the same place with the lights off · Where: tools/chiron_tiles.py, tools/make-chiron-tileset.py, apps/client/public/assets/tilesets/ChironDark.png · Learned: reuse the _source_ tiles of a world you know assembles correctly and recolour them — the wall corners, caps and shadow rows keep fitting, and only the palette changes

## The door between worlds is drawn as a dark doorway cut into the wall

What: Over each door rectangle there is a decorative two-tile `portal` sprite (from `ChironDark`) on the wall above it, plus a `threshold` glow on the floor tile; the First Office map embeds `ChironDark` just for those three tiles · Why: the client already draws a violet rectangle and the destination world's name over a door, but on its own that reads as a marker, not as a way out; a dark opening with cold light spilling from it is legible at a glance and says _which_ world is on the other side · Where: apps/client/public/assets/map/first-office.json (layer Furniture), tools/make-chiron-map.py (`portal`) · Learned: only the north wall of a room has a visible face in this art style — south and side walls are a one-tile line seen edge-on, so a doorway cut into them does not read; put the door in the north wall

## Sitting at a desk is a place in the map, not a feature of the app

What: "Being focused" is sitting at an object of class `seat` in the Tiled map (unique name, optional `dir`), and the whole state is one field, `Player.seatId`: focused _is_ holding a seat, and the status shown everywhere derives from it (`playerStatus()`) · Why: the alternative — a `focused` boolean plus a separate list of who occupies what — is two sources of truth that drift, and it would have put the office's furniture layout in code; with a seat as map data, adding a desk is adding an object in Tiled and seat occupancy comes free as room state, so whoever joins later sees who is sitting where · Where: packages/shared/src/map.ts (`CLASS_SEAT`, `findSeats`), packages/shared/src/schema.ts (`seatId`, `playerStatus`) · Learned: when a piece of state already answers two questions ("is this person focused?" and "is this seat taken?"), do not add a second field that can disagree with it

## Sitting down is the server's decision, unlike walking

What: `SIT` / `STAND` are requests; the server validates the seat, refuses one already taken, and writes `seatId`, position and facing itself — there is no rejection message, because a refusal is simply the state not changing and the client already draws from the state · Why: movement can stay client-authoritative (it is only ever about yourself), but a seat is contended: two people pressing the key at the same instant must not both get it · Where: apps/server/src/rooms/WorldRoom.ts (`onSit`, `onStand`) · Learned: the same app can have client-authoritative and server-authoritative state side by side — the dividing line is whether two clients can want the same thing

## A focus desk never marks you away by itself

What: the inactivity sweep skips anyone who is focused; only a manual "mark me away" can make a seated person away, and that frees the desk · Why: being heads-down without touching a key is exactly what a focus desk is for — evicting someone for using it as intended defeats the feature · Where: apps/server/src/rooms/WorldRoom.ts (`checkAway`, `onSetAway`) · Learned: an inactivity timer has to ask what the inactivity means before acting on it

## The chat's Tab and Enter are only taken when the keyboard is on the game

What: The chat's document-level shortcut takes `Tab` and `Enter`, and **both** are only intercepted when `document.activeElement` is the body or the canvas (and no modifier is held); from any control — the name field, the away button, the person card's buttons — they go on doing what the browser intends · Why: `isTyping()` alone is not enough of a guard: it only covers text fields, so a button with the focus would have had its `Tab` stolen and the panel would have become unreachable by keyboard. `Enter` originally kept only the `isTyping()` guard, which meant pressing `Enter` on any focused button both fired that button and stole the keyboard for the chat field — harmless while the only buttons were the away toggle, and immediately visible once the person card added four more · Where: apps/client/src/ui/chat.ts (`onGame`, the `document` keydown listener) · Learned: when a plain key is turned into a shortcut, the question is not only "is someone typing?" but "is the key already doing a job here?" — `Tab` walks the controls and `Enter` presses them, so both can only be borrowed from the one place that has no controls

## Each person's colour is hashed, then the clashes are resolved against the roster

What: `personColor.ts` gives every person one of eight hues by FNV-1a over their **sessionId**, and then resolves the collisions against everyone currently in the office: whoever is alone on their hue keeps it, and only those contending for one are settled, in order of session id, each walking forward to the next free hue · Why: the session is the key and not the name because the name is editable from the panel's own field and a colour that shifts mid-conversation defeats the point; and a bare hash is not enough on its own — into eight hues, three people in a bubble land on the same colour 34.5% of the time, which is precisely the case the colours exist for. Resolving against the roster gives 0 duplicates up to eight people, comes out identical on every client and after a reload (the roster and its order are the server's state, so the input is the same everywhere), and leaves 89% of the people already present on their colour when someone new arrives · Where: apps/client/src/ui/personColor.ts (`resolveColors`, `personColors`), consumed by presence.ts, bubble.ts and chat.ts · Learned: a "stable hash into N buckets" is a birthday problem, not a distribution problem — check the collision rate at the group sizes that actually matter before trusting it, and derive the fix from shared server state so every client computes the same answer without having to agree on anything

## The app is near-black with one indigo doing all the pointing

What: The whole chrome - HUD, entry screen, toasts, side panel, the card, and the world's own labels and bubble ring - sits on near-black (`--bg` #0b0b0e, `--panel` #17171b, `--card` #1a1a1e) with a single vivid indigo `--accent` #5b5bd6 as the only filled colour. The side panel's four parts each keep their own surface so the eye finds the edge between them without reading the titles: people list `--side-1` #141418, bubble `--side-2` #1e1e25, chat `--side-3` #0e0e11 (sunk, a canvas for the messages), one's own controls `--side-4` #08080a, separated by `--line-soft` seams rather than black borders · Why: the chrome steps back so the world, the people and their colours are what carry the app; and the accent had to be split in two, because #5b5bd6 carries white text at 5.4:1 but is only 3.4:1 as text on the panel - so `--accent-bright` #a5a6f6 is what names and focus rings are drawn in · Where: apps/client/src/style.css (`:root`), apps/client/src/main.ts (`backgroundColor`), apps/client/index.html (`theme-color`), Avatar.ts / BubbleArea.ts / SeatHint.ts for the in-world chrome · Learned: every accent- and status-derived tint is now written as `color-mix(in srgb, var(--token) N%, ...)` instead of a hand-mixed hex, so the next palette change is one edit and every tint stays measurable against the surface it actually sits on

## The person card is DOM floating over the canvas, not drawn into it

What: Clicking somebody - their avatar in the world or their row in the list - opens a card over their head with Wave, Chat, Go to them and an overflow menu. It is an HTML element inside `#game`, re-placed every animation frame with `transform` from `OfficeScene.anchorOf()`, and clamped inside the container (flipping below the feet when there is no room above) · Why: it is a piece of interface - text, buttons, a menu, a focus order - and all of that already exists in CSS, while in Phaser it would have to be rebuilt and would stop being reachable by keyboard; `transform` is used because it is the one property that moves an element every frame without laying the page out again · Where: apps/client/src/ui/personCard.ts, apps/client/src/ui/selection.ts, OfficeScene (`anchorOf`, `pickAt`) · Learned: the two places you can pick somebody from sit on opposite sides of the app and the scene is rebuilt on every trip through a door, so the selection lives in its own tiny store that both write to and neither knows about the other - nothing hung off the scene would survive the restart

## Waving ignores proximity, and the cooldown is held on both sides

What: `WAVE_SEND {to}` / `WAVE {from, name}`; the server relays to the named person alone, at any distance, refusing an unknown target, oneself, and anything inside the sender's cooldown (`WAVE_COOLDOWN_MS`, 3s). A refused wave gets no reply at all · Why: waving is how you catch the eye of someone across the room, so requiring a bubble the way chat does would leave it with nothing to do; and the client holds the same cooldown and greys the button out for it, so the only way to reach a server refusal is a client that ignored its own rule - there is nothing useful to tell it, and an error channel for it would be protocol nobody reads · Where: packages/shared/src/messages.ts, apps/server/src/waves.ts, apps/client/src/ui/personCard.ts · Learned: the wave is drawn by `Avatar.emote()`, a tweened glyph created for the occasion rather than a member of the container, so it never steals the speech balloon from somebody who is mid-sentence

## Walking to someone is A* over a grid read off the map

What: `buildGrid()` derives a walkable tile grid from what the map already knows - the colliding tiles of every collision layer plus each body in the `solids` group rasterised onto the tiles it covers - and `findPath()` is A* over it: 8-way, octile heuristic, refusing to cut corners, on a binary heap. `OfficeScene.walkTo()` follows the waypoints with the existing velocity and animation, stops 40px short (inside the 64px bubble radius, so the conversation opens without standing on them), and gives up if nothing has moved for 1.2s · Why: a walk-towards that slides along walls gets stuck behind the first desk, and the grid costs nothing to derive because it is read off the map rather than maintained beside it; tile granularity is safe since the avatar body is 18x12 inside a 32px tile · Where: apps/client/src/game/pathfinding.ts, OfficeScene (`walkTo`, `steer`) · Learned: the card's Chat button leans on it - in a bubble it focuses the field, and from across the room it walks you over and focuses it on arrival, which is what keeps a proximity-scoped button from being dead most of the time
