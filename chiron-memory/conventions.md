# convention

A rule the codebase follows — naming, patterns, and where things live.

## Room tile vocabulary of the office map

What: Rooms are drawn with a fixed set of FloorAndGround gids: vertical walls 152 (room on its right) and 92 (room on its left), 154 for the east outer edge; top wall 994; bottom wall 217 with corners 216/213 (218 on the east edge); room floor 2383 with 2319 as the first row under the top wall; corridor floor 414 (leftmost column) + 415 · Why: the map has no autotiling — picking the wrong variant leaves visible seams, and these gids are the ones the existing rooms already use · Where: apps/client/public/assets/map/oficina-taller.json (layers Piso and Paredes) · Learned: copy the tile vocabulary from an existing room rather than guessing tiles from the tileset image

## Doorways are holes in the wall layer, not special tiles

What: A door in a vertical wall is a two-tile gap in the Paredes layer with gid 788 as the cap right above it, and floor tiles underneath; in a horizontal wall it is a one-tile gap flanked by jambs 995 (left) and 993 (right) · Why: collision comes from the tiles themselves, so removing the wall tile is what makes the avatar able to walk through · Where: apps/client/public/assets/map/oficina-taller.json · Learned: a room is trapped exactly when its wall ring has no gap — nothing in code marks a doorway

## One spawn, and every zone reachable from it

What: The map keeps exactly one spawn object (reception); rooms never get their own spawn, and a test walks a flood fill from the spawn requiring at least one reachable tile inside every zone · Why: players must always land in reception and be able to reach any room on foot; the test is what catches a room walled off by accident · Where: apps/server/test/map.test.ts

## Map spawns: the unnamed one is the world entrance, named ones are door arrivals

What: A map has exactly one `spawn` object without a name (or named `spawn`) — the world entrance — plus any number of named spawns, each the target of a door in another map; optional `dir` gives the facing on arrival and `radius` the scatter · Why: doors have to name where they land, and the entrance has to stay unambiguous for people entering the world from the app · Where: packages/shared/src/map.ts, docs/mapa.md · Learned: put an arrival spawn outside the return door's area (and give it a small radius) or the traveller is sent straight back

## The Chiron Office plan: a gallery with open alcoves, not rooms off a corridor

What: 34×24 landscape canvas; an east-west `Galería` (rows 11-14) crosses the whole world and every zone opens onto it — `Vestíbulo`, `Los Monitores` and `Archivo` to the north, `El Pozo`, `Sala de mando` and `Café nocturno` to the south — separated by short stub walls (which stop before the gallery) and single-tile pillars, with no interior doors · Why: the two worlds have to be tellable apart at a glance, and the First Office is the opposite shape — a square 40×40 canvas of closed rooms hanging off a vertical corridor · Where: tools/make-chiron-map.py (`ZONES`, `STUBS_NORTH`, `STUBS_SOUTH`, `PILLARS`) · Learned: leaving the stubs short is what keeps the flood-fill test happy for free — every alcove has at least two ways in

## Chiron's map is generated by a pair of scripts that share a tile table

What: `tools/chiron_tiles.py` holds the ordered tile table (name → source gid, palette, collides) and both `make-chiron-tileset.py` (writes the PNG) and `make-chiron-map.py` (writes the map, naming tiles instead of gids) import it · Why: the tileset's tile order _is_ the map's gid order — if only one of the two scripts is re-run, the map silently points at the wrong tiles · Where: tools/chiron_tiles.py · Learned: re-run both, in that order; the furniture comes from the same packs as the First Office and some assemblies (meeting table, desk with PC) are copied from its map by tile rectangle rather than rebuilt sprite by sprite
