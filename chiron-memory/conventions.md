# convention

A rule the codebase follows — naming, patterns, and where things live.

## Room tile vocabulary of the office map

What: Rooms are drawn with a fixed set of FloorAndGround gids: vertical walls 152 (room on its right) and 92 (room on its left), 154 for the east outer edge; top wall 994; bottom wall 217 with corners 216/213 (218 on the east edge); room floor 2383 with 2319 as the first row under the top wall; corridor floor 414 (leftmost column) + 415 · Why: the map has no autotiling — picking the wrong variant leaves visible seams, and these gids are the ones the existing rooms already use · Where: apps/client/public/assets/map/first-office.json (layers Floor and Walls) · Learned: copy the tile vocabulary from an existing room rather than guessing tiles from the tileset image

## Doorways are holes in the wall layer, not special tiles

What: A door in a vertical wall is a two-tile gap in the Walls layer with gid 788 as the cap right above it, and floor tiles underneath; in a horizontal wall it is a one-tile gap flanked by jambs 995 (left) and 993 (right) · Why: collision comes from the tiles themselves, so removing the wall tile is what makes the avatar able to walk through · Where: apps/client/public/assets/map/first-office.json · Learned: a room is trapped exactly when its wall ring has no gap — nothing in code marks a doorway

## One spawn, and every zone reachable from it

What: The map keeps exactly one spawn object (reception); rooms never get their own spawn, and a test walks a flood fill from the spawn requiring at least one reachable tile inside every zone · Why: players must always land in reception and be able to reach any room on foot; the test is what catches a room walled off by accident · Where: apps/server/test/map.test.ts

## Map spawns: the unnamed one is the world entrance, named ones are door arrivals

What: A map has exactly one `spawn` object without a name (or named `spawn`) — the world entrance — plus any number of named spawns, each the target of a door in another map; optional `dir` gives the facing on arrival and `radius` the scatter · Why: doors have to name where they land, and the entrance has to stay unambiguous for people entering the world from the app · Where: packages/shared/src/map.ts, docs/map.md · Learned: put an arrival spawn outside the return door's area (and give it a small radius) or the traveller is sent straight back

## The Chiron Office plan: a gallery with open alcoves, not rooms off a corridor

What: 34×24 landscape canvas; an east-west `Gallery` (rows 11-14) crosses the whole world and every zone opens onto it — `Arrival Hall`, `Focus Desks` and `Archive` to the north, `The Pit`, `War Room` and `Night Café` to the south — separated by short stub walls (which stop before the gallery) and single-tile pillars, with no interior doors · Why: the two worlds have to be tellable apart at a glance, and the First Office is the opposite shape — a square 40×40 canvas of closed rooms hanging off a vertical corridor · Where: tools/make-chiron-map.py (`ZONES`, `STUBS_NORTH`, `STUBS_SOUTH`, `PILLARS`) · Learned: leaving the stubs short is what keeps the flood-fill test happy for free — every alcove has at least two ways in

## Chiron's map is generated by a pair of scripts that share a tile table

What: `tools/chiron_tiles.py` holds the ordered tile table (name → source gid, palette, collides) and both `make-chiron-tileset.py` (writes the PNG) and `make-chiron-map.py` (writes the map, naming tiles instead of gids) import it · Why: the tileset's tile order _is_ the map's gid order — if only one of the two scripts is re-run, the map silently points at the wrong tiles · Where: tools/chiron_tiles.py · Learned: re-run both, in that order; the furniture comes from the same packs as the First Office and some assemblies (meeting table, desk with PC) are copied from its map by tile rectangle rather than rebuilt sprite by sprite

## The avatar sheets' seated frames are 48 down, 49 left, 50 right, 51 up

What: the last four frames of the LimeZu 52-frame layout are the seated poses, one still frame per direction (no seated animation), and every composed-avatar layer (body per skin tone, hair style, top, trousers, shoes, facial hair, accessories) carries them too · Why: they were documented as "48-51: sitting, not used yet" with no order given, and the order is not guessable — it does not match the idle/walk order (right, up, left, down) · Where: packages/shared/src/avatars.ts (`SIT_FRAME`), apps/client/src/game/Avatar.ts (`setSeated`), tools/avatar_frames.py (`DIR_MAP`) · Learned: render the frames side by side and look at them; the layer sheets were split frame-for-frame, so anything true of the preset sheets is true of them, and the drawing tools have to take the order from one shared table — tools/gen-accessories.py had its own copy saying 48 right, 49 up, so seated avatars wore glasses drawn for the wrong direction

## The Chiron Office plan: an arrival hall you land in, focus desks next door

What: the north row of alcoves is `Arrival Hall` (cols 1-9, the doorway and the world's name in light on the wall), `Focus Desks` (cols 11-21, six workstations in two banks of three) and `Archive` (cols 23-32); the stub between the hall and the desks stops at row 6 so you can walk straight from where you land to a desk · Why: arriving used to give no sense of place and nothing to do; the mark and the return door now have to be on screen without moving, and the desks have to be findable from there · Where: tools/make-chiron-map.py (`ZONES`, `DESK_COLS`, `DESK_SEAT_ROWS`, `MARK_TILE`) · Learned: the desk banks leave a one-tile gap between units on purpose — those gaps are the way through from the gallery to the north wall, which is what keeps seated people out of the corridors

## Full-screen cards size to the viewport with one scrolling region

What: A card in a fixed full-screen overlay gets `max-height: 100%` and lays its children out as a flex column where the chrome (title, fields, the submit button) is `flex: none` and exactly one region is `flex: 0 1 auto; min-height: 0` with an inner `overflow-y: auto` scroller — so the card can never exceed the window and the action button can never leave it · Why: the entry card had neither, and since `body` has `overflow: hidden` nothing could scroll to reach "Enter the office" — people were locked out of the office on any short window · Where: apps/client/src/style.css (`.entry__card`, `.entry__avatars`, `.entry__panels`) · Learned: use `flex: 0 1 auto` (shrink but never grow) on the region, or tall screens get an empty scroll gap; pair it with a `@media (max-height: 640px)` block that trims padding and the optional subtitle

## The avatar generators check their own output, and there is a tool for looking at it

What: `tools/gen-accessories.py` ends by walking every silhouette against every hair style on all 52 frames and exits non-zero if hair escapes a crown, if the crown drifts off the head between frames, or if glasses leave the measured eye row or appear on a face turned away; `tools/contact-sheet.py` composes avatars into a labelled grid (`--rows`, `--cols`, `--frames idle|walk|sit`) in the order `appearanceLayers()` defines · Why: what breaks in these sheets is never visible in one frame of one avatar — a hat that fits ash sits inside adam's hair, a hat that fits short hair lets a bun through — so the unit of verification has to be the whole matrix, and the half of it that is a judgement about how something looks has to be rendered and looked at · Where: tools/gen-accessories.py (`verify`), tools/contact-sheet.py, tools/avatar_head.py · Learned: the contact sheet found bugs in itself twice while being used (it took the frame range and the column labels from the first row, so every row drew the first row's animation) — a verification tool is code and wants the same suspicion as the thing it checks
